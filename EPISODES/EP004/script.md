## Draft narration (one continuous paragraph)
Follow the rain on a window backwards far enough, and it leads to the sea. Sunlight warms the ocean, and water rises off it as invisible vapor. High up, the vapor cools into tiny droplets, and the droplets gather into clouds. Wind carries those clouds over land, and they fall as rain on that same window. Now drain the ocean overnight. Nothing rises from the empty seafloor. A little water still lifts off lakes, soil and plants, so the clouds wouldn't vanish at once. They'd thin out. Less cloud means less rain, and less rain means drier ground with even less water to give. A clearer sky sounds calmer. But clouds do a second job. It turns out they're the planet's sunshade, bouncing sunlight back to space before it lands, so with fewer of them, more sun would pour onto the bare ground. Hot, bright days, then cold, clear nights, like a desert. The sky was running on the ocean all along.

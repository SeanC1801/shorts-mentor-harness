| C2 | Followed back far enough, the rain on land leads to the sea. | 1 | fact / interpretation | [USGS Water Science School, "Evaporation and the Water Cycle"](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/evaporation-and-the-water-cycle) (about 90% of atmospheric moisture evaporates from oceans, seas, lakes and rivers); archived [USGS "Water cycle summary"](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090305041328/http:/ga.water.usgs.gov/edu/watercyclesummary.html) (over land, precipitation routinely exceeds evaporation; about 10% of ocean evaporation falls on land) | Much land rain is recycled from land evaporation (40–60% depending on the study, e.g. [HESS 2023](https://doaj.org/article/ce754d1076b94f59b92c9013b40dbd1d)), so a single drop's last stop is often land. "Far enough" carries this: land gets more rain than it evaporates, and the extra comes in from the sea. Script does **not** say "most rain comes from the ocean". |

| C5 | Wind carries clouds over land, where they fall as rain. | 4 | fact (simplified) | archived [USGS "Water cycle summary"](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090305041328/http:/ga.water.usgs.gov/edu/watercyclesummary.html) (ocean moisture transported over land falls as precipitation) | Strictly, moist air moves and clouds form and re-form along the way; "clouds carried by wind" is the standard simplified picture. |

| C9 | **Twist:** clouds act as the planet's sunshade, reflecting sunlight back to space; with fewer clouds, more sunlight would reach the ground. | 11, 12 | fact | [NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, "CERES Cloud Radiative Effect"](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/30603) (clouds reflect solar energy, which cools, and trap infrared, which warms; satellite observations show the net is cooling); [NOAA GFDL, "Cloud Radiative Effect"](https://www.gfdl.noaa.gov/cloud-radiative-effect/) (shortwave about −50 W/m², longwave about +30, net about −20 W/m²); L'Ecuyer et al. 2019 via [Stony Brook](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/reassessing-the-effect-of-cloud-type-on-earths-energy-balance-in-/) (net −17.1 ± 4.2 W/m²) | **Replaces the weak Wikipedia source flagged in idea v3.** The script says only that more sun would reach the ground, not that the planet would be hotter overall: an ocean-less Earth would also lose water vapor (a greenhouse gas) and change in other ways, so the global temperature result is not known. - "The planet would get hotter"; not supported for an ocean-less Earth (see C9).
- Where the ocean's water goes; the premise says it simply disappears.

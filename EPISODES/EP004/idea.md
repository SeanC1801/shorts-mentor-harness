## Concept
- **Central question:** Where does rain come from, and what would happen to the sky if the ocean were gone?
- **One payoff:** the sea and the sky are one machine. Take away the sea and the sky runs dry. **Closing twist (creator decision 2026-11-10):** the thinning clouds are also a sunshade, so with fewer of them more sunlight would reach the ground. The guess most people make (fewer clouds, cooler or calmer) runs the other way.
- **Hook promise:** a rainy window, one simple question, then one chain of "and then": no ocean, nothing to evaporate, thinning clouds, drier land.
- **Audience assumption:** curious viewers of any age; no science needed.
- **Emotional shape:** curiosity (where does rain come from?) → "oh, that's how it works" (sea to sky to rain) → the ocean drains and the same path runs empty → reversal (the clouds were also a sunshade) → honest uncertainty → reflective close.
- **Opening image:** a kid at a rainy window (or a beach), one focal point. Illustrative, not final: "A kid watches rain on a window. Where did it come from?"
- **Visual opportunity:** the camera rides one drop up from the sea, into a cloud and down as rain. Then the ocean drains and the same path runs empty: no drop rises, the cloud thins, the ground dries. In the last beat, sunlight pours through the thinned sky onto bare ground. Day-lit, then a bright, empty sky (two lighting states).
- **Tone:** curious and lightly humorous; the creator can change this.

## Assumption stated once, plainly, in the script
The ocean's water simply disappears overnight; it does not go anywhere we must explain, so the story stays on what follows. Like EP003's rock-ring assumption.

## What the sources support (accessed 2026-11-10; web searches only, none independently verified)
- Most evaporation and rain happen over the ocean: a classic figure is about 86% of global evaporation and 78% of precipitation; newer satellite estimates say about 75–85% and about 70%. [Schmitt 1995 review (WHOI); AGU GPCP study] — script uses "most", not a percentage, unless a better source is found.
- About two-thirds of Earth is under clouds at any time (NASA MODIS 2002–2015). [NASA Earth Observatory]
- Oceans cover about 71% of the surface and hold about 97% of Earth's water. [UNESCO Ocean Literacy] — only if the script needs it.
- Land plants and soil also release water (about 58% of land rain is recycled land evaporation in one 2023 study). [Hydrology and Earth System Sciences, 2023] — so clouds would thin out, not vanish at once; **the script must say "thin out" unless the research shows otherwise.**
- **Twist source:** clouds net *cool* the planet today (about 44 W/m² of sunlight blocked against about 31 W/m² of heat trapped; net about 13 W/m²). [Wikipedia "Cloud feedback"] — **weak source; verify with CERES or IPCC before the script is approved.** Low clouds mostly cool, high clouds mostly warm. This is a statement about clouds alone; a real ocean-less Earth would change other things too (humidity, heat storage), so the spoken line stays at "less shade, more sunlight", not "the planet would be hotter".

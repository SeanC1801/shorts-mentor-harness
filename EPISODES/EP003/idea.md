# EP003 — Concept card v2 (DRAFT, awaiting creator approval)

**Pillar:** Scientific What-If Scenarios
**Creator seed (verbatim, 2026-11-02):**
> What If Earth Had Rings Like Saturn?
> Hook: "Imagine looking outside your window and seeing THIS instead of the normal sky."
> Visual: A character opens the curtains to discover enormous planetary rings overhead.
> Payoff: Explain how the rings' appearance would change with location and how their shadows could affect the planet, depending on the rings' properties.

**Creator decision (2026-11-02):** a mix of framing A ("Look out the window") with C ("Earth may have had one") as the ending, from concept v1. B (the shadow) is not selected; keep it as a possible follow-up.
**Creator note (2026-11-02):** the audience goal is curiosity-driven content that also appeals to younger viewers, not only adults.

## Concept
- **Central question:** What would the sky look like from where you live if Earth had a ring, and did Earth ever really have one?
- **One payoff:** the ring would look different, or be hidden, depending on where you stand. The ending beat: a 2024 study suggests Earth may really have had a ring about 466 million years ago.
- **Hook promise:** one ordinary morning, one impossible sky, then a quick tour of what people in other places would see.
- **Audience assumption:** curious viewers of any age; no astronomy needed.
- **Emotional shape:** awe (the window) → playful "what about over there?" tour → surprise (not everyone would see it) → "wait, really?" (it may have happened) → honest uncertainty.
- **Opening image:** a child opens bedroom curtains; the sky holds a bright stripe. One focal point.
- **Visual opportunity:** the camera leaves the window and travels to a beach at the equator (a thin line), a mid-latitude city (a huge arch) and a far-north town (low, or hidden); day and night give two lighting states. The ending shows an asteroid stretching into a ring and crater dots lighting up along the equator on a globe.
- **Tone:** curious and lightly humorous; the creator can change this.

## Assumption stated once, plainly, in the script
"Like Saturn" cannot be literal: ice would not last at Earth's distance from the Sun, so the ring is assumed to be rock, which looks darker than Saturn's. It sits in Earth's equatorial plane, a few Earth radii out.

## What the sources support (accessed 2026-11-02; web searches only, none independently verified)
- Saturn's rings: mostly water ice, particles from sand-grain to mountain size, typically about 10 m thick in the main rings. [NASA Goddard, "The Rings of Saturn" (Simpson)]
- Rings sit inside the Roche limit; for icy material around Earth, roughly 18,000 km from Earth's centre (about 9,000–10,000 km above the surface). [Wikipedia, "Roche limit"; Science Notes]
- A ring in the equatorial plane would arc over the equator and be visible day and night; icy rings would likely vaporize at Earth's distance from the Sun, so a rock ring is more plausible. [Science ABC; Discover Magazine] (popular-science sources)
- Ending: Tomkins, Martin & Cawood (2024), "Evidence suggesting that Earth had a ring in the Ordovician", *Earth and Planetary Science Letters* 646:118991. 21 impact craters, concentrated near the equator, from a 466-million-years-ago impact spike; asteroid roughly 10.5–12.5 km across; cooling is speculative and untested; other experts are not convinced. [Sky & Telescope; EarthSky] — must be told as "a study suggests".

## Open items for the script gate (research, not claims yet)
1. **Ring size and brightness** from a source, for the chosen rock-ring assumption.
2. **View by latitude:** my own geometry suggests a ring at about 2.5 Earth radii would be hidden north of roughly 66° latitude, and look like a thin line from the equator. Verify in a sky simulator and with an expert before stating it.
3. **Earth's axial tilt (about 23.4°)** if mentioned: cite a source.
4. **Ordovician paper:** read the paper itself and at least one expert critique; confirm crater count, dates and what the authors say about cooling.
5. Whether to mention shadows at all (framing B). Default: no, one payoff only.

## Mentor notes (critique, not approval)
- **Hook:** the seed line withholds "THIS", which can read as bait. A flat opening is better, for example (illustrative; the creator chooses in the script): "A kid opens the curtains on a Tuesday morning. There is a stripe across the sky."
- **Ending risk:** the Ordovician ring is one study. Keep the line hedged and end on the open question, not on a conclusion.
- **Length:** one tour of three places plus the ending is the maximum; more places repeat the same beat.
- **Look (visual style v2):** bedroom with foreground curtains, mid stage, painted sky backdrop; ring is a textured arc; day and night grading.
- **Younger viewers (judgment, unmeasured):** a child character, a big image in the first second, quick location jumps, and a simple question ("would you see it?").

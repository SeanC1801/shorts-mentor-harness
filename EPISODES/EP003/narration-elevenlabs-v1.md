# EP003 narration for ElevenLabs (from approved script v1)

Paste only the text in the box below into ElevenLabs. It is the approved script v1 narration word for word, with each line as its own paragraph. Do not change wording here; any wording change is a new script version that needs re-approval.

```text
A kid opens the curtains on a Tuesday morning. There's a stripe across the sky.

It's a ring, like Saturn's, only rock. Ice wouldn't last this close to the Sun.

At the equator, it's a thin line overhead. Farther north, a wide arch. Far enough north, it never clears the horizon.

Now the strange part: Earth may really have had one.

Around 466 million years ago, a study suggests, an asteroid passed too close, and Earth's pull tore it apart into a ring.

The clue: twenty-one craters from that era all formed near the equator.

Not everyone agrees. But if the study is right, Earth once wore a ring, long before the dinosaurs.
```

## Delivery notes
- **Tone:** curious, clear, lightly playful. Line 1 is the anti-hook: say it flat and matter-of-fact, as if describing an ordinary morning.
- **Pace:** brisk, with short pauses of about a fifth of a second after each sentence, never more than about a third. Trim long gaps after generating instead of slowing the voice.
- **Pronunciation to check by ear:** "466 million" as "four hundred sixty-six million"; "twenty-one" as words.
- **Rule-of-three line** (line 3: "At the equator... Farther north... Far enough north...") reads best as three even beats with a slightly longer beat on the last.
- **Line 4** is the turn: a small lift in curiosity, not a shout.
- **Last line:** underplay it; a short pause before "But if the study is right" is fine.
- **Audio tags** (like `[thoughtful]` or `[short pause]`) are your choice; they shape delivery only and must not change the spoken words.

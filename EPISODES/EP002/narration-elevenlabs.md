```text
The U.S. government once paid to teach pigeons to aim.

Here's the setup: in the nose, a pigeon watches a screen showing the target.

The bird pecks. The image shifts. The controls move.

One pigeon is a gamble, so the plan used three, and the majority would win.

This was World War Two, and the idea came from psychologist B. F. Skinner.

And here's what nobody expected: in simulations, the trained birds kept the target centered.

So why isn't this how missiles work? In 1944 it was cancelled so the military could focus on weapons it could use sooner.
```

## Delivery notes
- **Tone:** curious, clear, lightly dry. Line 1 is the anti-hook: say it flat and matter-of-fact, not hyped.
- **Pace:** brisk. Keep pauses short, about a fifth of a second after each sentence and never more than about a third. Trim long gaps after generating rather than slowing the voice.
- **Pronunciation to check by ear:** "B. F. Skinner" (letters B, F), "1944" as "nineteen forty-four", "U.S." as letters.
- **Rule-of-three line** ("The bird pecks. The image shifts. The controls move.") reads best as three even beats.
- **Last line** lands the joke by underplaying it; a short pause before "Nobody wanted..." is fine.

# Storytelling guide
1. Open with an **anti-hook** (creator's standing preference): a flat, understated, concrete line that withholds the explanation and raises a question. It must be honest and give just enough context to picture the scene.
2. Layer context gradually, one new piece per beat, so curiosity stays open until the story is complete. Orient early enough that the viewer is never lost; if a beat is confusing, add context before adding mystery.
3. Escalate with evidence, contrast, or a surprising mechanism.
4. Use a visual explanation or emotional turn, not narration alone.
5. Pay off the opening promise; end cleanly, optionally with a relevant open question.

Narration craft (attributed in `source-analysis/narration-style-notes.md` and `narration-style-notes-ricks-clips.md`): short plain sentences, one idea each, one pivot into the explanation, cause-effect chain, end on the consequence, hedge uncertain claims.

Anti-hook check: Does line 1 create a specific question? Is there one clear image? Does each later beat answer something and open or deepen something? Is the promise honest and paid off? Whether anti-hooks outperform other hooks is unverified; treat as the channel's chosen style, not a proven rule.

Retention critique looks for confusion, repeated information, delayed context, flat emotional beats, and visuals that merely decorate speech. Do not treat platform metrics folklore as fact. The mentor proposes edits; creator selects.

## Art and look (as described)
1. **Characters are simple primitives.** A ball for a head, a bean for a body, thin stick limbs. A big head carries the acting.
2. **Faces are swapped, painted decals** on the head (brows, eyes, mouth), drawn from one master sheet so every expression looks like one family. Roughly 40+ expressions plus extras (sweat drops, tears, anger vein). Open eyes know where the lid and pupil are, so characters can blink and look at what they touch.
3. **Two skin options:** per-character skin tones, or a classic all-white head and hands. The white heads get extra lift so they don't turn gray under light.
4. **Dense, dressed sets.** A set never starts from an empty floor; props are plentiful and specific to the story. Recurring places are built once and reused.
5. **Rendering look ("cinema"):** soft toon shading with ink outlines, warm rim light, strong depth of field, glow, optional film grain. A flatter "clean" variant exists for diagram-heavy explainers.
6. **Light rigs by mood:** warm interior, neon, underwater, space.
7. **Facts become objects:** a rolling counter, a gauge with a small overshoot, flipping calendar pages, sweeping clock hands, a magnifying glass sliding to the line being read. Every spoken number gets a physical on-screen event on its word.
8. **The main character is "you"** in a second-person story.

## Motion rules (as described)
- **Holds are dead still.** No idle bounce or sway; life comes from camera, blinks, face swaps, and props.
- **Gestures land before the word.** A short gesture (about 5 frames) finishes 3 to 5 frames before the word it belongs to; the head keeps turning 2 to 4 frames after the hand stops.
- **Faces swap through a blink** (a half-closed frame, a closed frame, then the new face), about 2 frames before the stressed word, roughly every 1.2 s for the lead. Mouths do not lip-sync; the face does the acting.
- **The camera is almost always moving** and smooth, with no handheld shake, creeping slowly between moves and speeding up into transitions.
- **Near-zero hard cuts:** shots join with a short dissolve (about 6 frames, 0.2 s) starting on the first word of the next sentence, with both shots still moving. Special transitions: flying through a door, zooming into an eye.
- **Pace:** a new picture about every 1.1 s (about every four words); about one shot per clause; hook pays off in 2 to 3 s; many shorts loop so the last line runs into the first.
- **Checks worth borrowing:** no hands through heads, feet planted, nothing floating or sliding; faces stay out of the caption band (captions sit near 70% of height); close-ups may crop the top of a head but never the eyes; each shot shows what the words say when they say them; no empty corners.

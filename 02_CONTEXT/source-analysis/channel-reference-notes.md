# Channel reference notes: topic, title, and hook patterns

Selective, attributed observations from the public Shorts tabs of two channels, viewed 2026-10-31. They inform general coaching questions and seed brainstorming; they are not a persona, script, or title bank to copy.

**Evidence limits:** Only titles, public view counts, and channel taglines were read. Narration, scripts, pacing, and visuals were **not** analyzed. View counts are public but say nothing about causes, retention, or revenue, and results vary widely within each channel (roughly tens of thousands to tens of millions of views). Treat everything below as hypotheses. To deepen this note, the creator can supply transcripts, which should be read selectively and summarized here with title and video ID.

## The Why Side (@WatchTheWhySide)

Tagline: "Small questions. Big reveals." About 35K subscribers, 37 videos at time of viewing.

Observed patterns (from titles):
- **Everyday object, hidden mechanism:** a pen, a windshield, a Rubik's Cube, a washer, a mouse. Suits the *weird technology* seed.
- **Direct address and stakes:** "Your windshield...", "Your jeans are...", "Never hold this...", "A power line just fell on your car." The viewer is placed inside the situation.
- **Plain question or what-if:** "Why does...?", "What if you drank the Dead Sea?", "Could you run on water like this lizard?" Suits the *scientific what-if* seed.
- **Strange human cases:** a medical oddity, a surprising test, an expert-fooling animal. These ran from modest to the channel's highest reported views, so no single pattern clearly wins.

Possible lesson (unverified): a small, concrete question can carry a Short if the reveal is genuinely explained.

## Ricks Clips (@RicksClips3000)

Self-description: a creator who enjoys voiceovers. About 121K subscribers, 145 videos at time of viewing.

Observed patterns (from titles):
- **Concrete object or place plus an unresolved "why":** storm oil, a rigged horse race, a town built around a button, a grave bell, an ancient jar. Suits the *strange history* seed.
- **Paradox, loophole, or contest framing:** an unstoppable spear vs an unbreakable shield; a knight's loophole. Strong visual-conflict potential.
- **Human behavior and probability angles:** random numbers, lottery patterns, cashiers. Often paired with a story hook.
- **Short noun-led titles** that name the oddity without revealing it.

Possible lesson (unverified): a title that names a strange concrete thing creates a gap the story must close honestly.

## How the three seeds map

| Seed | Useful questions the references suggest |
|---|---|
| A. Weird technology | What mundane object hides a surprising mechanism? Who first built it and what did they assume? |
| B. Strange history | What odd object, place, or rule had a real purpose we've forgotten? What changed because of it? |
| C. Scientific what-if | What single physical change would the viewer instantly picture, and what is the unexpected consequence? |

## Boundaries

- Do not reuse a title, premise, script structure, voice, catchphrase, or visual identity from either channel. Use them only to ask better questions about original topics.
- Do not claim these patterns cause views. Do not promise virality.
- Keep the creator's premise and every approval central. Any topic suggestion needs the creator's explicit selection.
- Narration style for this channel stays defined by `channel-identity.md` (curious, clear, lively, lightly humorous), not by a reference creator.

# Animation style notes: Ricks Clips (@RicksClips3000)

Selective visual observations from frames sampled while playing two public Shorts in the browser on 2026-10-31. They inform original design decisions; they are **not** a character, layout, or branding template to copy.

**Sources:** "The Town Built Around a Button" (`ZDy3xXA2iHk`, 4 frames) and "How a Mirror Rigged a Horse Race" (`5WMQhPObNqQ`, 3 frames).

**Evidence limits:** Seven still frames from two videos. I could not see motion, frame rate, pose timing, or the production tools used, so nothing here says how the animation is made or whether it uses stepped poses. Narration was not analyzed. The small caption line at the top of frames is likely YouTube's own subtitle overlay, not part of the design.

## Observed patterns
1. **Simple characters in rich environments.** Characters have round, bean- or egg-shaped heads, tiny dot eyes, thin limbs, flat colors, and a dark outline. Backgrounds are far more detailed, painted or rendered, with real perspective and depth. The contrast keeps attention on characters while giving the world a lot of detail.
2. **Role shown by small accessories.** A hat, bow tie, or overalls identifies a character at a glance; faces are readable through eyelids, brows, and mouth shape (a smirk, open laughter).
3. **Scale and perspective for drama.** Tiny figures against huge spaces (a ring of houses, a tall wall with a ladder) and a mix of wide establishing shots with close-ups on a face.
4. **Light as story.** Glows, radial light, and cast light rays signal mystery or a reveal. Color mood shifts with the scene: night blues and warm glows versus sunny greens.
5. **Information carried by props and signage.** Odds boards and sign text in the scene convey context without extra narration.
6. **Captions:** large, bold white word-by-word text with a dark outline, centered in the lower half, one word at a time.
7. **Saturated, bright palette,** consistent with a clean, colorful look.

## Use in this channel
- Treat as supporting evidence for the 2.5D direction: flat expressive cutout characters over layered, detailed backgrounds with parallax-ready depth.
- Plan one wide establishing frame and at least one close-up per Short; use scale contrast for stakes.
- Put key facts on in-scene props (a screen, a sign, a stamp) where a viewer can read them.
- Keep word-by-word captions large, high contrast, and clear of faces and key action.

## Boundaries
- Do not copy his character shapes, accessories, color combinations, scene layouts, or caption styling as a branded look. Create original characters (`character-registry.md` requires creator approval) and our own design system.
- Do not infer production methods or frame rates from stills. Our motion rules (`motion-rules.md`) stay as specified.
- Do not claim this look drives views.

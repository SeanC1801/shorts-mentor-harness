## 1. How they appear to choose topics

### Where a topic starts
| Starting point | Examples | Views |
|---|---|---|
| A thing millions already care about, plus a "what could go wrong" | GTA 6's release date → could it black out the planet? | 919K |
| A famous name in a live rivalry, told as "you" | Elon Musk, Google, Facebook in the AI race | 2.59M, 454K, 611K |
| Fresh news inside a fan community | Fortnite's Pirate Shop token odds | 53K, 13K |
| An odd true story with a twist | the chicken-thief hawk; the looping Waymo | 16K, 22K |
| A big timeless question about "you" | living forever; life after death; simulation | 62K, 50K, 58K |
| Their own comment section | "you guys kept saying you'd just buy a generator" | 12K |

### Principles the videos share
1. **The subject comes first, the science second.** They start from something the viewer already has feelings about (a game, a billionaire, death) and then bring in the facts. They do not start from an interesting fact and look for an audience.
2. **Timing.** The GTA 6 video went up 13 days before the release it is about. The Fortnite video followed odds that had "just" been posted. The chicken story is "from 2026".
3. **The title is the first line.** "Could GTA 6 Black Out the Whole Planet?" opens "The whole planet could shut down on November 19th." The stakes are in both, in about ten words.
4. **Second person.** "You're Elon Musk." "Imagine you are Google." "You want to find out what happens when we die."
5. **A big claim, backed by real precedents, then an honest answer.** The blackout video cites 2003, Texas 2021 and India 2020, then ends "Probably not." The hook is large but the video does not lie.
6. **A question for the comments in the description.** "Who will win the AI race? Share your pick."
7. **Volume.** Six videos in a week each. One clear hit per channel; the rest are 12K–62K.
8. **A production tool.** Afterhour Theory's descriptions credit "Creatorize Suite" for animation and music. That is the paid app described in `animation-style-notes-3d-cartoon-reference.md`, and it helps explain 3-minute 3D videos every two days. This matters for the efficiency plan, not for topics.

### How they expand an idea
| Move | Example | Result against the original |
|---|---|---|
| **Clone the frame, swap the subject** | Elon Musk (11-06) → Google (11-08) → Facebook (11-10) | 18% and 24% of the hit, still their 2nd and 3rd best |
| **Sequel on the same subject, answering comments** | GTA 6 blackout → "your generator won't save you" | 1% of the hit; the channel's weakest |
| **Follow the same news again** | Pirate token → official odds posted | 25% of the first |
| **Move to a neighbouring big question** | death → immortality → simulation | all similar (50K–62K) |

What this suggests: after a hit, **the frame travels and the subject does not.** A new famous subject in the same frame held up; a second helping of the same subject did not.

## 2. Our workflow against that

### What already works (keep)
| In the harness | Why it holds up |
|---|---|
| One central question per Short | Every reference video does this |
| Honest promise; claim ledger; sources kept in `research.md` | Their best video also deflates its own claim honestly |
| The creator owns the premise and every approval | Nothing in the evidence argues against it |
| Narration audio sets the timing | Sound practice; unrelated to topic |
| `branch-test-plan.md` and `learnings.md` | The channel is already running topic tests and recording results |
| The 2026-11-11 learning: what-if in the first sentence, one idea per Short | The reference data supports it directly |

### What the evidence argues against (change candidates)
| Rule today | Where | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Use an anti-hook in every script": understated, context-light opening | `me.md`, `storytelling-guide.md` step 1 | Both hits open with the stakes stated outright. The guide itself says anti-hooks are unverified. EP004 held 63% against EP003's 78%. |
| The title "carries the second half of the promise; do not restate it" | `storytelling-guide.md`, story chain 1 | Their title and first line say the same thing |
| Reveal in the last 15–20% of the words | story chain 7 | Their turn comes near the middle (the hawk reveal is at 31 s of 63 s) |
| "No questions to the viewer"; nothing after the callback | story chain | They ask questions and loop the ending |
| One continuous paragraph where each sentence answers the last | story chain | This produced 12-word sentences; theirs average 6.5–9 |
| "Under a minute when the story supports it" | `channel-identity.md` | Their videos run 63–177 s |
| Flat cutout characters, poses held 2 frames, camera always moving, word-by-word captions | `me.md`, `production-standards.md`, `document-contracts.md` | Superseded by the creator's 2026-11-13 direction (3D characters) and by the caption and cut findings |

### What is missing
1. **A topic stage before the idea card.** `/short-ideate` begins once the creator has a seed, and develops three framings of that one seed. Nothing asks whether strangers already care about the subject, whether it is timely, or what the title and first line would be.
2. **Title and first line before research.** Today the title is suggested after the preview is approved (the 2026-11-13 rule). The references suggest the title is the first decision, not the last.
3. **A plan for what to do after a result.** There is no rule for following a video that does well with a clone of its frame.
4. **Comments as a source.** Nothing reads our own videos' comments for seeds.
5. **Balance of effort.** EP004 went from approved idea to its first approved storyboard in under three hours, then took about two more days to produce. Its handoff records about 20 approvals; one of them covered the topic. The evidence says the topic is where a 50-fold difference comes from.

## 3. Proposed topic workflow (for the creator to accept or change)
1. **Collect seeds, weekly.** Ten to fifteen one-line seeds from five sources:
   - the creator's own curiosity
   - what is timely in the next two to four weeks (releases, launches, anniversaries, seasons)
   - comments on our own videos
   - a clone of our best video's frame with a new subject
   - big "you" questions in the untested science branches
2. **Five quick checks per seed** (yes or no, one minute each):
   - Does a stranger already care about the subject before we say anything?
   - Can the stakes be said in ten words that work as both title and first line?
   - Can it be told as "you"?
   - Is there a chain of at least six distinct pictures?
   - Is there an honest answer with sources we can find?
3. **Write the title and first line for the top three.** The creator picks one, rewrites it, or rejects all three.
4. **Idea card** for the chosen seed, as now, with the title and first line on it. Then the existing gates: script, storyboard, preview.
5. **After posting:** record views, stayed-to-watch and average view at 24 hours and 7 days. If a video clearly beats the channel's usual result, the next seed list starts with clones of its frame.
